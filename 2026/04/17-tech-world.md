## Tech & AI

### Anthropic Releases Claude Opus 4.7

Anthropic launched **Claude Opus 4.7**, its newest and most capable model, on April 16. The release comes amid intense scrutiny of the company's earlier **Mythos** model, which demonstrated alarming cyber-offensive capabilities and was restricted from general release. Opus 4.7 is described as having "weaker cyber skills than Mythos" -- a deliberate design choice as Anthropic navigates the tension between capability and safety.

Bloomberg reports that the Mythos situation has **Wall Street scrambling**: banks, tech giants, and governments are trying to understand what the model could do in adversarial hands. The Economist's cover story this week asks whether **five men (Dario, Demis, Elon, Mark, and Sam)** should control the Western world's most potent technology, arguing that a laissez-faire approach to AI is "no longer politically tenable or strategically wise."

### Snap Cuts 1,000 Jobs, Blames AI

Snapchat parent Snap announced **1,000 layoffs** (16% of its workforce), explicitly citing "rapid advancements in artificial intelligence." CEO Evan Spiegel told employees the cuts would save **$500 million** by H2 2026. Snap joins a growing list -- roughly **30,000 layoffs have been blamed on AI** so far in 2026, according to Challenger, Gray & Christmas.

### Forbes AI 50 List: The State of AI Startups

Forbes released its **eighth annual AI 50 list**, featuring juggernauts like OpenAI and Anthropic with a combined **$242.6 billion in venture funding**. There are 20 newcomers including Chai Discovery (AI drug development). The data labeling space is increasingly competitive: Mercor, Scale AI, Invisible Technologies, Surge, Turing AI, micro1, and Handshake are all racing for market share. Meanwhile, **Mercor's 23-year-old billionaire founders** are dealing with employee fraud and suspected North Korean operatives infiltrating the company using stolen credentials.

### Netflix Earnings Beat, Hastings Steps Down

Netflix beat Q1 expectations ($12.25B revenue, +16% YoY) but the stock fell on soft Q2 guidance. In a major leadership change, **co-founder Reed Hastings is stepping down** as board chair, ending a 28-year association with the company he built from scratch.

### Allbirds Becomes an AI Company

In one of the most dramatic corporate pivots in recent memory, footwear company **Allbirds sold its entire shoe business** to American Exchange Group and rebranded as "NewBirdAI" -- an AI compute and cloud services company. The stock surged **582%** on the announcement. The pivot encapsulates the current AI gold rush where even the most unlikely companies are trying to ride the wave.

## World News

### Lebanon-Israel Ceasefire Goes Into Effect

A **10-day ceasefire** between Israel and Hezbollah in Lebanon went into effect on Thursday, brokered by the U.S. Displaced residents began driving back to their villages. The ceasefire appears to be holding as **European leaders prepare to meet** to discuss the broader Strait of Hormuz situation.

### Iran Deal "Looking Good"

Stock markets continue to price in an Iran deal, with multiple signals suggesting negotiations are progressing. The **Strait of Hormuz closure** continues to impact global energy markets, food prices, and shipping, but the diplomatic momentum has shifted sentiment from fear to cautious optimism. The Economist warns that millions will go hungry if the strait stays closed, as expensive fuel and fertilizer lead to smaller harvests.

### Trump's Washington Triumphal Arch

A federal agency has **approved the concept** for Trump's planned triumphal arch in Washington D.C. Separately, a federal judge **blocked above-ground construction** on the controversial $400 million White House ballroom, allowing only below-ground work to proceed.

### Hungary's New Era

Peter Magyar's election victory continues to reverberate. The incoming prime minister faces a **fragile macroeconomic situation** and Hungary is being watched as a test case for reversing democratic decay after years of Viktor Orban's rule.

### Tech Jobs Bust: Real But Not (Yet) AI's Fault

The Economist reports that the **tech jobs bust is real** -- technology firms are shedding workers at a notable rate. However, the publication argues the blame shouldn't be placed entirely on AI yet. Rising interest rates, post-pandemic normalization, and efficiency drives are more immediate causes, though AI displacement is accelerating.
