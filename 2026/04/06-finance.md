In a Wall Street Journal interview published late Sunday, President Trump warned he could strike **"every power plant"** in Iran if a ceasefire deal isn't reached. This comes as the **Strait of Hormuz deadline looms on Tuesday**, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments.

Meanwhile, **Reuters reports that Iran and the US have both received a plan** to end hostilities with an immediate ceasefire. Japan continues mediating.

**Why it matters for your wallet:** Oil has been the dominant force in markets for weeks. WTI spiked above $114 intraday but pulled back to ~$111. ## 💊 Deal of the Day: Neurocrine to Buy Soleno for $2.5B+

**Neurocrine Biosciences** (NBIX) is close to acquiring **Soleno Therapeutics** (SLNO) for over $2.5 billion, per the Financial Times. Soleno's stock jumped **more than 30% overnight**.

**What does Soleno do?** They make the first commercial treatment for extreme hunger (hyperphagia) caused by **Prader-Willi syndrome**, a genetic disorder affecting people from childhood. Soleno reported its first quarterly profit in Q4 after its treatment became the company's first approved medicine.

This is a classic biotech acquisition pattern: small company gets FDA approval → revenue ramps → big pharma acquires at a premium.

## 🔍 Bond Market Tug of War

The Economist highlighted the battle happening in bond markets: **10-year Treasury yields are yoyoing** as traders pull in opposite directions.

- **Inflation fears** (Iran war, oil prices) → push yields UP
- **Recession fears** (economic slowdown, uncertainty) → push yields DOWN

This volatility is hitting everything from mortgage rates to corporate borrowing costs. The 10-year Treasury yield is the most important number in global finance right now.

## 🎓 Concept of the Day: Stagflation

**What is it?** Stagflation is when the economy experiences **stagnant growth AND high inflation at the same time**. It's the worst-case economic scenario because the usual fix for inflation (raising interest rates) makes the growth problem worse, and the usual fix for stagnation (cutting rates) makes inflation worse. The Fed gets trapped.

**Why it matters today:** The Economist notes that bond market volatility reflects "increasing risk of an unpleasant economic outcome: high inflation with stagnant growth." We're seeing exactly this risk:
- **Inflation rising** → March CPI expected at 3.3% (up from 2.4%), driven by Iran war energy costs
- **Growth uncertain** → Business confidence shaken by geopolitical tension, oil shocks, and bond volatility

The last major stagflation episode was the 1970s, driven by — you guessed it — an oil crisis in the Middle East. History may be rhyming.
