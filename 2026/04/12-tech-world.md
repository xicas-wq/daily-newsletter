## Tech & AI News

### SoftBank Launches New High-Performance AI Firm
SoftBank and partners have set up a **new company focused on developing high-performance AI** systems, continuing Masayoshi Son's aggressive push into artificial intelligence. The venture aims to build frontier AI models competitive with US leaders like OpenAI and Anthropic. This follows SoftBank's massive $100B+ Stargate investment earlier this year.

### Microsoft VP: AI Layoffs Will Increase Demand for Workers
In a counterintuitive take, Microsoft VP **Rajesh Jha** argued that even if companies lay off half their workforce due to AI, it would ultimately **increase demand for workers**. His reasoning: AI dramatically lowers the cost of creating products and services, which expands markets and creates new roles that don't exist yet. The classic "automation creates more jobs than it destroys" argument — though this time the scale of disruption may be different.

### Russia Holds Meeting on AI Development
Putin convened a meeting on AI technology development, signaling Russia's intent to **compete in the AI race** despite sanctions limiting access to advanced chips. The meeting focused on domestic AI model development and military/industrial applications.

### Anthropic's Mythos Model Raises Safety Concerns
The Economist reports on **Mythos**, Anthropic's latest AI model, and warns that CEO Dario Amodei's safety concerns **should not be dismissed**. The article examines the growing gap between AI capabilities and the guardrails meant to contain them.

### VisionClaw Gets Music Generation
In the BabelCloud ecosystem, VisionClaw merged support for **music generation via Google's Lyria 3** model through the Gemini tool — agents can now generate music alongside text, code, and images.

## World News

### Iran Ceasefire Under Strain
The fragile US-Iran ceasefire that boosted markets this week is showing cracks. Iran's Tasnim news agency says **"excessive" US demands** are hindering a lasting agreement. Key sticking points include:
- US insistence on nuclear inspections
- Iran's demand for sanctions relief
- The future of the Strait of Hormuz (through which 20% of world oil flows)

If talks collapse, oil could spike back to $100/barrel and global markets would take a hit.

### Oil Ceasefire Impact on Global Markets
The ceasefire sparked uncertainty in oil markets. Brent crude has pulled back from highs but remains elevated. Analysts are split on whether the ceasefire will hold — the consensus is that any deal will be **temporary and fragile**, keeping a risk premium on oil prices for months.

### Europe Faces War-Driven Economic Crisis
Europe is grappling with **cascading economic effects** from Middle Eastern instability: higher energy costs, disrupted trade routes, and refugee pressures. Manufacturing in Germany and Italy has been hit particularly hard by energy price spikes, and the ECB is weighing whether to cut rates to offset the slowdown.

### IMF/World Bank Spring Meetings
This weekend's IMF and World Bank meetings in Washington are dominated by the **economic shock of the Middle East conflict**. Key topics:
- Emergency lending for oil-importing developing nations
- Global growth forecast downgrades
- Food security concerns as fertilizer and grain prices rise

### Indonesia's Prabowo Meets Putin
Indonesian President Prabowo is meeting with Putin to discuss **geopolitics and energy deals**, highlighting the growing alignment between Southeast Asian nations and Russia as they seek cheap energy amid global market disruption.

### Venezuela's Opposition Pitches Oil Reforms
Venezuelan opposition leader pitched **oil sector reforms** at CERAWeek in Houston, seeking international support for restructuring the country's oil industry — a move that could eventually bring significant supply back online.
