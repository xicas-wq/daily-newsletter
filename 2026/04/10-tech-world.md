### Tech & AI

**1. Meta Moves Toward AI-Built Software**

Meta is reassigning engineers to a new AI unit focused on making autonomous agents do most of the work of building, testing, and shipping software. This is a major signal that Big Tech sees AI not just as a product but as a replacement for traditional software engineering workflows.

**2. Nvidia's Jensen Huang: Put AI Breakthroughs Before Profit**

Jensen Huang urged companies to prioritize AI breakthroughs over immediate payoffs. At GTC last month, he introduced the Nvidia Agent Toolkit for building AI agents in enterprises and predicted every SaaS company will need to become an "AaaS" (Agent-as-a-Service) company. When seat counts decline, companies that can charge for output instead of headcount will win.

**3. Anthropic Rattles Cybersecurity Industry**

Anthropic's new model release can detect flaws in operating systems, causing CrowdStrike and Cloudflare to drop 7%+. Reuters also reports Anthropic is exploring designing its own chips, which could reduce dependence on Google and Amazon's silicon. The software sector is down 25.5% this year as AI disruption fears intensify.

**4. Big Tech Backs Nuclear Power for AI**

Major tech companies are pouring money into next-generation nuclear technology to power AI data centers. The energy demand from AI workloads is so massive that companies are looking beyond traditional power sources.

**5. IQM Opens First US Quantum Technology Center**

Finnish quantum computing company IQM opened its first US facility in Maryland. Separately, researchers published a study claiming exponential quantum advantage in certain machine learning tasks, and a new proposal for quantum-safe Bitcoin (without a hard fork) was published.

**6. What Is "Claude Mythos"?**

Forbes reports on Anthropic's mysterious Claude Mythos — a model the company reportedly won't let anyone use. The article is trending as one of Forbes' most-read pieces this week.

### World News

**1. Iran Ceasefire on Fragile Ground**

The two-week US-Iran ceasefire is holding but tensions are rising fast:
- **VP Vance** headed to Pakistan this weekend to negotiate a longer-term deal, warning Iran not to "play" the US
- **Trump** threatened Iran on Thursday, saying "The 'Shootin' Starts'" if they don't hold up their end
- **Israel-Lebanon:** Netanyahu authorized direct negotiations with Lebanon, expected to start next week in DC. But Israeli strikes killed 300+ in central Beirut on Wednesday, straining the ceasefire
- **World leaders skeptical:** UK's Starmer questioned Trump's values; EU foreign policy chief Kallas warned Israeli attacks on Lebanon are "putting the ceasefire under severe strain"
- **Strait of Hormuz:** Iran charging $2M tolls, traffic below 10% of pre-war levels

**2. Melania Trump Denies Jeffrey Epstein Ties**

The First Lady issued a rare public statement denying close ties to Jeffrey Epstein, following renewed scrutiny.

**3. US Fertility Rate Hits Record Low**

The number of births per 1,000 women of reproductive age fell ~1% last year — an all-time low, down nearly 20% from two decades ago. Experts say declining birth rates act as an economic "drag" by reducing demand and labor supply. The pronatalist movement hasn't reversed the trend.

**4. Disney Layoffs Under New CEO**

Disney is cutting up to 1,000 jobs under new CEO Josh D'Amaro, primarily in marketing. The company faces challenges around stock price, franchise fatigue, and linear TV's decline, though parks and cruises remain strong.

**5. DOJ Probing NFL's TV Deals**

The Justice Department opened an investigation into whether the NFL uses anticompetitive tactics that harm consumers through its complex web of TV and streaming rights deals. The NFL defends its model but critics say its 1961 antitrust protections are outdated in the streaming era.

**6. Emperor Penguins Declared Endangered**

Emperor penguins were officially declared endangered as Antarctica's sea ice continues to shrink — a stark climate change milestone.

**7. Artemis II Splashdown Tonight**

The Artemis II astronauts are scheduled to splashdown off the California coast tonight around 8pm ET after orbiting the Moon — reentering at ~25,000 mph.
