## Tech & AI

### OpenAI Unveils GPT-5.4-Cyber
OpenAI released **GPT-5.4-Cyber**, a new AI model focused on cybersecurity applications, just one week after a rival's competing announcement. The model is designed for threat detection, vulnerability analysis, and security automation. The rapid release pace highlights the intensifying AI arms race.

### NVIDIA Launches Open-Source Quantum AI Models
NVIDIA released open-source quantum computing AI models to help advance the intersection of quantum and classical computing. The models aim to make quantum computing more accessible to researchers and developers working on complex optimization problems.

### Google Gemma 4 Runs Natively on iPhone
Google's **Gemma 4** open model can now run with full offline AI inference directly on iPhones — no cloud connection needed. This is a significant milestone for on-device AI, suggesting a future where powerful AI runs locally on your phone without sending data to the cloud.

### Anthropic's Mythos AI Model Raises Safety Questions
The Economist highlighted concerns about Anthropic's new AI model **Mythos**, asking "How dangerous is it?" The model made their most-read list, reflecting growing public interest in AI safety as models become increasingly powerful.

### Meta Facial Recognition in Ray-Ban Glasses Draws Backlash
Activists are urging Meta to cancel facial recognition features in its Ray-Ban AI glasses. Privacy advocates say the technology could enable mass surveillance in public spaces, reigniting the debate over biometric technology in consumer devices.

### News Organizations Block the Wayback Machine
23 major media outlets (including USA Today and NYT) are now blocking the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine from saving their web pages — fearing AI companies will use archived snapshots to train language models without paying. The Internet Archive's director says they have controls to prevent large-scale scraping, and is in talks to regain access.

## World News

### Lebanon and Israel Hold Historic Direct Talks
Lebanon and Israel engaged in their **first direct diplomatic dialogue in over 30 years** in Washington, DC. The talks aim to lay groundwork for future negotiations as Israeli strikes in Lebanon have displaced over 1 million civilians (per the UN). Hezbollah has said it will **not abide** by any agreements reached. Meanwhile, Iran and the US could meet for another round of talks as early as this week.

### Nuclear Arms Race Warning from IAEA Chief
Rafael Grossi, head of the International Atomic Energy Agency, gave a sobering interview warning that **if a nuclear arms race starts, it will be unstoppable**. The Iran war has intensified fears about nuclear proliferation, with countries potentially seeking their own nuclear deterrents. The Economist called it their top story of the day.

### Hungary's Democratic Renewal
Following recent elections, political observers note that Hungary's process of **democratic renewal has only just begun**. The country's shift away from Viktor Orban's authoritarian-leaning government faces significant structural challenges, with comparisons being drawn to the post-Soviet transitions of the 1990s.

### Trump's Crypto Policies Undermining US Power
Project Syndicate published a widely-shared analysis arguing that Trump's pro-crypto agenda is a "geopolitical Ouroboros" — while promoting dollar-pegged stablecoins domestically, the policies have expanded infrastructure used to **evade American sanctions**. Illegal crypto transactions reportedly increased over 160% in 2025, largely driven by Russia, Iran, and North Korea.

### India's Battle for Female Voters
A massive battle for women's votes is reshaping Indian elections, with politicians offering direct cash transfers to women. Observers worry about the proliferation of such populist spending schemes and their impact on fiscal responsibility.

### American Luxury Fashion Booming
From Ralph Lauren to The Row, American luxury brands are having a moment. Five years ago, zero American brands were in the top ten of the hottest fashion labels. Now three make the cut, marking a cultural shift in global fashion.
