## LEAD: Iran Rejects Peace Talks, Hormuz Crisis Deepens

The US-Iran standoff escalated sharply over the weekend, but markets are taking it in stride (so far). Here's the latest:

**What happened:**
- **Iran rejected a second round of peace talks**, saying the US naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz constitutes a ceasefire violation (Forbes, DW)
- **US Navy seized an Iranian-flagged cargo ship (Touska)** that attempted to sail through the blockade. Trump confirmed the seizure on Sunday (AP News, Washington Post)
- Iran vowed a response, with officials warning the blockade will come at **"significant costs for everyone"** (Forbes)
- The **ceasefire expires Wednesday, April 22** -- a critical deadline

**Oil market reaction:**
- **Brent Crude surged past $96/barrel** on Monday, recovering nearly all of Friday's 13% crash (when Iran had briefly reopened the Strait)
- **WTI Crude jumped above $88**, up ~6% (Motley Fool, Reuters)
- Oil prices are now expected to rise 15% by June if the Strait remains closed (Crypto Briefing analysis)
- S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures fell ~0.5% pre-market, but actual opening losses are milder

**The whiplash timeline:**
- **Friday:** Iran opened Strait -> oil crashed 13% -> stocks soared to all-time highs
- **Saturday:** Iran reversed, reimposed restrictions
- **Sunday:** US seized Iranian ship, Iran rejected talks
- **Monday:** Oil surges, stocks barely flinch

**Why stocks aren't crashing:** The market appears to be pricing in a resolution. Despite the heated rhetoric, investors seem to believe the ceasefire deadline Wednesday will force both sides back to the table. This is a **very risky bet** -- if talks truly collapse, expect a much sharper correction.

**The Information** reported over the weekend that Google (Alphabet) is in discussions with **Marvell Technology** to build two new AI chips:
1. A memory device to speed up Google's TPU (Tensor Processing Unit)
2. A brand new AI-targeted TPU

Marvell stock surged **+5.05% to $146.75** -- hitting a new 52-week high of $148.77 intraday.

**Why it matters:** Google TPUs are Alphabet's strategy to challenge Nvidia's dominance in AI processors. Marvell handles chip design while TSMC handles fabrication. The new chips could be ready for test production early next year. This is part of a broader trend: hyperscalers (Google, Amazon, Microsoft) are all building custom silicon to reduce dependence on Nvidia.

## CAPE Tariff Refund Portal Launches Today

The government's new **Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE)** portal goes live today, designed to process **$127 billion in tariff refund claims** following the Supreme Court's February ruling that Trump's tariffs were unlawful.

**Key details:**
- Only 20% of eligible importers have completed the necessary steps to file claims
- **Consumers cannot use the portal** -- only importers can file
- Over 1,000 companies have sued for refunds (FedEx, Costco, and more)
- 90% of the tariff burden fell on US companies/consumers (NY Fed study)
- Small businesses face the biggest hurdle -- the system requires digitized records many don't have

**Bottom line:** Don't expect lower prices at the store. Even if companies get refunds, the labor costs of navigating the claims process mean savings are unlikely to be passed to consumers.

## Concept of the Day: "Oil Shock"

An **oil shock** is a sudden, sharp increase in oil prices that disrupts the broader economy. We're potentially watching one unfold right now.

**How they work:** Oil is embedded in virtually everything -- transportation, manufacturing, food production, plastics, heating. When prices spike suddenly, costs ripple through the entire supply chain. Companies face higher input costs, consumers pay more at the pump and grocery store, and economic growth slows.

**Historical examples:**
- **1973 Arab Oil Embargo:** OPEC cut production, oil quadrupled from $3 to $12. Result: deep recession, 12% inflation.
- **1979 Iranian Revolution:** Oil doubled. Result: stagflation (stagnant growth + high inflation).
- **2022 Russia-Ukraine War:** Brent briefly hit $130. Result: 40-year high inflation globally.

**Why it matters now:** Brent Crude at $96 and rising is approaching the danger zone. If the Strait of Hormuz stays closed past Wednesday's ceasefire deadline, analysts project $100+ oil and potentially much higher. The Strait handles ~20% of global oil traffic. Unlike previous shocks where supply was merely reduced, this time a major shipping lane is *physically blocked* -- a more acute disruption. The resilience of stocks today may be masking the real risk: if oil sustains above $100 for weeks, expect it to show up in gas prices, airfare, food costs, and eventually, corporate earnings.
