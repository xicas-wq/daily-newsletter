### Top Stories

#### 1. Strait of Hormuz Reopens — Markets Erupt, Then Uncertainty Returns

Friday was a blockbuster day on Wall Street. Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz open to all commercial shipping, responding to a US-brokered ceasefire that paused Israel's campaign in Lebanon. The reaction was immediate and dramatic:

- **Oil futures plunged as much as 13%**, dropping below $90/barrel for the first time in over a month
- **Airline stocks surged 6%+** on hopes that jet fuel shortages would ease
- **The Dow rallied 868 points** — its best day in weeks
- **The S&P 500 closed above 7,100 for the first time ever**

President Trump said on Truth Social that he expects a deal with Iran "within a day or two," with Iran reportedly agreeing to suspend its nuclear program indefinitely.

**But Saturday brought a twist:** Iran reimposed restrictions on Hormuz passage, citing US "breaches of trust" and continued US blockade of Iran-affiliated traffic. Multiple sources (CNN, Al Jazeera, The Guardian, Reuters) report the strait has effectively been shut down again. This reversal could rattle markets when they reopen Monday.

**Why it matters:** An estimated 135 million barrels of oil sit in tankers stranded in the Persian Gulf. The on-again, off-again status of Hormuz creates enormous uncertainty for energy prices, airlines, and the broader economy.

### Concept of the Day: Geopolitical Risk Premium

**What is it?** A geopolitical risk premium is the extra return that investors demand — or the extra cost built into commodity prices — because of political instability, military conflict, or diplomatic uncertainty.

**How does it work?** When the Strait of Hormuz was closed, oil prices spiked because traders priced in the risk that supply would be disrupted for a long time. When Iran reopened it Friday, that premium collapsed — oil fell 13% in a single day. But Saturday's reversal shows why risk premiums exist: the situation is genuinely uncertain.

**Connecting to today:** Friday's massive rally was partly about removing the geopolitical risk premium from energy prices. But Iran's Saturday reversal means that premium could snap right back on Monday. This is exactly why commodity traders say "the trend is your friend until the geopolitics isn't."

**Key takeaway:** Geopolitical events can move markets more dramatically than earnings reports or economic data. When you see oil or airline stocks making big moves, ask yourself: is this fundamentals, or is this a risk premium being added or removed?
