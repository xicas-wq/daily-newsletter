## Concept of the Day: Short Squeeze

**What is it?** A short squeeze happens when investors who bet a stock will fall (short sellers) are forced to buy shares to cover their losses, driving the price even higher.

**How it works:**
1. Short sellers borrow shares and sell them, hoping to buy back at a lower price
2. If the stock rises instead, short sellers face unlimited potential losses
3. As losses mount, they're forced to buy back shares (cover their position)
4. This buying creates more upward pressure, forcing even more short sellers to cover
5. The result: explosive, self-reinforcing price spikes

**Today's example:** Avis Budget Group. With 86% of available shares shorted, any positive movement triggered a massive cascade of forced buying. Short sellers lost approximately $5 billion. This is different from the 2021 GameStop/AMC meme stock rallies, which were driven by retail investors coordinating on Reddit -- Avis's squeeze was more mechanical, driven by pure positioning imbalance.

**Why it matters:** Short squeezes show how market mechanics can amplify price moves far beyond what fundamentals justify. The stock can soar even while the company reports billion-dollar losses. Understanding this helps you distinguish between "the stock went up because the company is doing well" vs. "the stock went up because of market plumbing."
