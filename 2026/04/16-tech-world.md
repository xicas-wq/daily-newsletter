## Tech News

### Snap Cuts 1,000 Jobs, Says AI Now Writes 65% of Its Code

Snapchat's parent company is laying off 16% of its workforce (~1,000 people) and closing 300 open positions. CEO Evan Spiegel cited "rapid advancements in artificial intelligence" as a key factor, saying AI is enabling smaller teams to move faster. The startling stat: **AI agents are already writing 65% of Snap's code.** The restructuring is expected to save $500M in the second half of 2026. Snap's stock rose ~8% on the news.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the clearest examples yet of AI directly replacing human jobs at scale — and the market rewarding it. Other tech companies have made similar moves (Google, Meta, Microsoft have all cited AI efficiency in recent layoffs), but Snap's 65% code stat is the most concrete metric we've seen.

### Allbirds Rebrands as "NewBirdAI" — Shoe Company Goes Full AI Pivot

In peak 2026 energy, sustainable shoe brand Allbirds announced it's pivoting to become an AI compute company. The plan: raise $50M, buy GPUs, and rent compute to AI companies. Stock surged 876%. The pivot comes after Allbirds sold its shoe business for $39M — a fraction of its $4B IPO valuation. Analysts are skeptical given $50M is a drop in the bucket compared to companies like CoreWeave ($35B capex this year).

### Starbucks Tests ChatGPT Commerce App

Starbucks is piloting an app within ChatGPT as OpenAI experiments with commerce integrations. The goal: let users discover and order from Starbucks through conversational AI. It's an early signal of how AI chatbots could become shopping interfaces.

### Anthropic and OpenAI "Locking Up" Their Latest Models

The Economist reports that both Anthropic and OpenAI are increasingly restricting access to their most capable models — requiring enterprise agreements, limiting API access, or keeping frontier capabilities behind closed doors. This trend raises questions about the "open" in OpenAI and the broader accessibility of cutting-edge AI.

## World News

### US-Iran Ceasefire Extension Under Discussion

The US and Iran are reportedly weighing an extension of their current ceasefire to allow more time for peace talks. Markets reacted positively — the S&P 500 and Nasdaq hit record highs on the news. However, tensions remain high:

- **Australia announced increased military spending** specifically citing the Iran war's global impact.
- **Oil prices eased** on the ceasefire hopes, giving relief to global markets.
- **Luxury brands suffering:** Hermès and Kering (Gucci's parent) reported the war is hurting sales.

### Trump vs. the Fed: Constitutional Showdown Looms

President Trump threatened to fire Fed Chair Jerome Powell if he doesn't resign when his term ends May 15. The DOJ sent investigators to the Fed's headquarters renovation site unannounced — and they were turned away. The Supreme Court is expected to rule as soon as tomorrow on whether the president can fire Fed governors, a decision with massive implications for central bank independence globally.

### Live Nation Found Guilty of Running an Illegal Monopoly

After a five-week trial, a Manhattan jury found that Ticketmaster owner Live Nation illegally monopolized the concert ticketing market and overcharged fans. Nearly 40 states brought the case after the DOJ settled. The states will now push for a potential breakup of the company — separating Ticketmaster from Live Nation's concert promotion business. Live Nation stock dropped 6%.

### LIV Golf May Lose Saudi Backing

Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund is reportedly on the verge of pulling its ~$5B investment from LIV Golf. The league, which lured PGA stars with massive paydays, has been losing money consistently. An announcement could come today. If confirmed, this likely spells the end of LIV Golf.

### SantaCon President Arrested for $1M Charity Fraud

The president of the annual Christmas-themed bar crawl SantaCon was arrested after federal prosecutors alleged he stole more than $1M from ticket sales meant for charity.

### Spirit Airlines on the Brink

Spirit Airlines may begin liquidation proceedings as early as this week, according to multiple reports. The budget carrier has been struggling since its failed merger with JetBlue was blocked by regulators.

### Hungary's Political Transition

Project Syndicate highlights Hungary's new leadership, warning the new leader must avoid mistakes similar to those made during the Biden administration — specifically around policy continuity and coalition management in an increasingly polarized Europe.
