## Consumer Mood: Record Low

The University of Michigan's Consumer Sentiment Index hit a **record low** in the final April reading. This measures how everyday Americans feel about the economy, their personal finances, and future expectations.

Why does this matter? Consumer spending makes up about 70% of the US economy. When people feel pessimistic, they tend to spend less, which can slow economic growth. However, consumer sentiment and actual spending do not always move together -- people sometimes say they feel bad about the economy but keep spending anyway.

## Big Idea: Will AI Deliver the Productivity Revolution?

Economist Carl Benedikt Frey (Project Syndicate) argued this week that AI's productivity boost **will not match the computer revolution** of the 1990s-2000s. His reasoning: the computer revolution transformed nearly every white-collar job simultaneously. AI, by contrast, is replacing specific tasks rather than overhauling entire workflows. Worth keeping in mind as you watch tech earnings this week -- are these companies actually making money from AI, or just spending on it?

## Concept of the Day: PCE vs. CPI -- Two Ways to Measure Inflation

You will hear "PCE" a lot this week. Here is what it means and why it matters.

**CPI (Consumer Price Index)** and **PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures)** both measure how fast prices are rising, but they work differently:

| | CPI | PCE |
|---|---|---|
| **Who makes it** | Bureau of Labor Statistics | Bureau of Economic Analysis |
| **What it measures** | Fixed basket of goods | Shifts with what people actually buy |
| **Example** | If beef gets expensive, CPI still tracks beef | If beef gets expensive and people switch to chicken, PCE tracks the switch |
| **Housing weight** | ~36% | ~16% |
| **Who watches it most** | Media, wage negotiations | The Federal Reserve |

**Why the Fed prefers PCE:** Because it adjusts for substitution (when people switch to cheaper alternatives), the Fed considers it a more accurate picture of real-world inflation. When the Fed says its target is 2% inflation, it means 2% on the PCE measure.

**This Thursday's PCE report** is expected to show 3.5% year-over-year -- still 1.5 percentage points above the Fed's target, which is why rate cuts remain off the table for now.
