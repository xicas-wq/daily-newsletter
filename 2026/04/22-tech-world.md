## Tech & AI

### 1. Apple CEO Transition: John Ternus Takes the Helm Sept 1
The biggest tech story of the week continues: Tim Cook will step down as Apple CEO after 15 years, with hardware SVP **John Ternus** taking over. Ternus is a mechanical engineer who joined Apple in 2001 and oversaw AirPods, the Mac chip transition, and the popular MacBook Neo. The key question: can a hardware guy lead Apple's AI strategy? CNN reports his appointment signals a "significant shift" -- Apple is betting that AI's future is on-device, and Ternus recently reorganized hardware engineering for faster AI product development. Products on deck: a smarter Siri, foldable iPhone, AI wearables, and smart home devices.

### 2. Florida Opens Criminal Investigation into OpenAI
Florida AG James Uthmeier announced a criminal probe into whether ChatGPT helped the gunman who carried out the mass shooting at Florida State University last year plan his attack. Uthmeier alleged "ChatGPT offered significant advice to the shooter" including weapon and ammunition recommendations. OpenAI called the shooting a "tragedy" but said the chatbot only "provided factual responses" available across public sources. This is one of the most significant legal challenges AI companies have faced regarding content responsibility.

### 3. Palantir Calls for Reinstating the U.S. Military Draft
Palantir CEO Alex Karp published a 22-point manifesto calling on the U.S. to reinstate the military draft, pursue AI-powered weapons, and push for Germany's remilitarization. The manifesto argues Silicon Valley has a "moral debt" to the country. The timing is notable given the ongoing Iran conflict and growing debate about military readiness.

### 4. Google Readying New AI Chips to Challenge Nvidia
Bloomberg reports Google is preparing to release new AI chips, escalating its competition with Nvidia in the AI hardware space. As AI compute demand continues to surge, multiple tech giants are developing custom silicon to reduce dependence on Nvidia's GPUs.

### 5. Big AI Congressional Race Heats Up
CNN spotlights a candidate for Congress who calls themselves "Big AI's #1 target," speaking out about battling AI CEOs and the growing influence of AI companies in politics. The story highlights the increasing intersection of AI policy and electoral politics.

## World News

### 1. Iran Ceasefire Extended but Fragile
Trump extended the Iran ceasefire indefinitely after Pakistan requested more time. However, hours later, Iranian state media claimed Tehran seized two ships in the Strait of Hormuz and attacked a third -- directly testing the fragile truce. The U.S. military blockade continues. Singapore's Foreign Minister warned the Hormuz disruption is "just a dry run" for larger geopolitical supply chain threats.

### 2. Germany Slashes Growth Forecast
Germany's Economy Ministry halved its 2026 growth forecast and raised inflation projections, citing the Iran war's energy impact. France's fiscal watchdog separately warned that economic turbulence could derail deficit reduction plans. Europe is emerging as one of the biggest economic casualties of the conflict.

### 3. Democratic Rep. Cherfilus-McCormick Resigns
Rep. Sheila Cherfilus-McCormick (D-FL) resigned from Congress amid a House ethics investigation, becoming the third representative to quit this month. The string of resignations is narrowing Democrats' already thin House margin.

### 4. Warsh Fed Confirmation Faces Roadblock
Kevin Warsh's path to Fed chair hit a snag as Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) vowed to block the nomination until the DOJ drops its probe of current Fed Chair Jerome Powell. Warsh promised independence under oath, saying Trump "never asked him to commit to lowering rates." But Trump told CNBC he'd be "disappointed" if Warsh doesn't cut rates immediately -- a stark contradiction that underscores concerns about Fed independence.

### 5. Artemis II: Earthset from the Moon
On a lighter note for Earth Day: Artemis II commander Reid Wiseman released spectacular iPhone footage of "Earthset" -- watching Earth disappear behind the moon from the far side. The footage went viral as a reminder of humanity's renewed presence at the moon.
