## Top Stories

### 1. Inflation Hits 2-Year High at 3.3%
Consumer prices rose **3.3% year-over-year in March**, the highest reading in two years, according to BLS data released Friday. The spike was largely driven by surging gas prices from the Iran conflict. However, **core inflation** (stripping out food and energy) grew just 0.2% month-over-month — suggesting the underlying trend isn't as alarming as the headline number.

**Why it matters:** The Fed is now in a tough spot. A top Fed official this week floated the possibility of a **rate hike** if gas-driven inflation persists, reversing the rate-cutting trajectory markets were counting on. Consumer sentiment has fallen to a record low as Americans worry about war-driven price increases.

### 2. Anthropic's "Claude Mythos" Sends Banks Into Emergency Mode
An impromptu meeting of major bank CEOs (Bank of America, Citi, Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo) with Fed Chair Jerome Powell and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was held this week after Anthropic released its latest AI model, **Claude Mythos**. The model can detect cybersecurity vulnerabilities with exponentially greater efficiency than human hackers.

Officials believe Mythos could debilitate Fortune 100 companies, infiltrate national defense systems, and take down large chunks of the internet. Human security teams find about 100 critical flaws per year — Mythos can find **thousands**. Anthropic limited the release to ~40 organizations and briefed government officials before distribution.

## Concept of the Day: CPI (Consumer Price Index)

**What is it?** The CPI measures the average change in prices paid by consumers for a basket of goods and services over time. It's the most widely watched measure of inflation.

**How does it work?** The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) tracks prices of ~80,000 items monthly — everything from eggs to electricity to haircuts. These are weighted by how much the average household spends on each category. The result is a single number that tells you how much more (or less) expensive life has gotten.

**Why are there two versions?**
- **Headline CPI** includes everything, including food and energy
- **Core CPI** excludes food and energy because they're volatile (war can spike gas prices overnight, weather can spike food prices)

**This week's example:** March CPI was 3.3% (headline) — sounds scary. But core CPI grew only 0.2% month-over-month. The difference? Gas prices surged because of the Iran war, pulling the headline number way up. Core CPI tells you the "underlying" inflation trend without those temporary shocks.

**Why the Fed cares:** The Fed targets 2% inflation. At 3.3% headline, they're well above target. But they know gas prices might fall if the ceasefire holds, so they're watching core CPI more closely before deciding whether to hike rates.
