## Tech & AI

### Anthropic's Claude Mythos: Too Powerful for Public Release
The biggest tech story of the week: Anthropic's latest AI model can find cybersecurity vulnerabilities thousands of times faster than human hackers. The company limited distribution to ~40 organizations and briefed government officials before release. An emergency meeting with bank CEOs and the Fed followed. Meanwhile, Anthropic is fighting a Pentagon designation as a "supply chain risk" after limiting military use of its AI — highlighting the growing tension between AI companies and the defense establishment.

### Starbucks Rolling Out AI Chatbots
Starbucks is deploying AI chatbots at cafe locations, with analysts calling it a "litmus test" for the restaurant industry. CEO Brian Niccol's "Green Dot Assist" initiative aims to use AI for order-taking and customer service. If successful, expect fast-food and retail chains to follow.

### Japan Bets $16 Billion on AI
Japan is making a massive $16 billion investment to propel its position in the AI race, signaling that the competition for AI leadership is becoming a global government priority, not just a Silicon Valley venture capital play.

### Sam Altman's Home Attacked
A suspect was arrested for allegedly throwing a Molotov cocktail at OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's home. An exterior gate caught fire but no injuries were reported. The attack reflects growing public tensions around AI leadership and the tech industry.

### AI "Human-Only" Slack Channel
An AI startup created a dedicated "human-only" Slack channel where its 20 real employees can chat without triggering the company's AI employees to generate unnecessary tasks. A humorous but telling sign of the friction in AI-augmented workplaces.

## World News

### Iran Ceasefire Holds — But Talks Off to Rocky Start
VP JD Vance is in Pakistan for US-Iran peace negotiations. The two-week ceasefire remains in place, but Iran set preconditions before talks could begin: release of frozen assets and a ceasefire in Lebanon. Vance warned that if Iran tries to "play us," the negotiating team "is not that receptive."

**European fallout:** European airports could face jet fuel shortages within three weeks if the Strait of Hormuz remains contested. The war's economic toll is piling onto Europe's existing political stress, with the NYT reporting that the economic pain is compounding across the continent.

### Artemis II Returns Safely
NASA's Artemis II crew splashed down safely off San Diego's coast Friday evening — a "perfect bull's-eye splashdown." Astronauts Reid Wiseman, Christina Koch, Victor Glover, and Jeremy Hansen completed a record-breaking lunar mission. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman declared the US is "back in the business of sending astronauts to the moon."

### Xi Jinping Meets Taiwan Opposition Leader
Chinese President Xi Jinping met with the leader of Taiwan's main opposition party — the first such meeting in nearly a decade. The diplomatic gesture comes amid broader geopolitical realignment, with a NYT analysis pointing to growing "strategic autonomy and multipolarity" in the emerging global security framework.

### Taylor Swift & Travis Kelce Wedding
Save-the-dates have gone out: Taylor Swift and Travis Kelce are reportedly planning to tie the knot in NYC over the Fourth of July weekend — competing with the nation's 250th birthday celebrations.
