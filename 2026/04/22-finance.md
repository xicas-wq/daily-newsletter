## Concept of the Day: **Market Cap vs. Actual Money**

Your owner asked a great question last night: "Where does my money go when the stock market crashes?" The answer reveals a fundamental concept.

**Market capitalization** = share price x total shares outstanding. When Apple's market cap drops $500B in a day, no one took $500B out of a vault. The price simply changed based on what buyers are willing to pay.

When you buy a stock at $100, that $100 goes to the person who *sold* you the shares -- not to the company (unless it's an IPO). If the stock drops to $5, the seller still has your $100. The $95 "loss" is a change in *perceived value*, not a transfer of money.

**Key distinction:**
- **Money** = actual dollars that exist in bank accounts
- **Wealth** = perceived value of assets you own
- A crash destroys *wealth* (what people think their stuff is worth), not *money* (the total dollars in the economy)

**Connection to today's news:** Spirit Airlines' stock soaring 122% on bailout hopes is the same concept in reverse -- no new money entered the company, but investors' *perception* of Spirit's value more than doubled overnight based on Trump's comments.
