## Concept of the Day: Strait of Hormuz

**What is it?** The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman — only about 21 miles wide at its narrowest point. It connects the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the open ocean.

**Why does it matter for markets?** Roughly **20-25% of the world's oil** passes through this strait daily. When Iran closed it five weeks ago at the start of the current conflict, oil prices spiked approximately 70%. That's because there's no easy alternative route for all that oil — tankers from Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, and the UAE all depend on it.

**The connection to today**: Trump's Tuesday deadline is specifically about reopening this strait. If Iran doesn't comply and military strikes follow, oil prices could spike further — raising gas prices, increasing inflation, and hurting consumer spending. If a deal is reached, expect oil to drop and markets to rally. This is why traders are so laser-focused on tonight's deadline.

**Key takeaway**: Geography can drive markets. A 21-mile-wide waterway can move trillions of dollars in global markets because of how concentrated oil shipping routes are.
