### Top Stories

**1. Iran War's Economic Damage May Be "Irreversible"**

The IMF warned it will lower its 2026 global growth forecast (currently 3.3%) to account for permanent economic losses from the conflict, even if peace holds. Key ongoing impacts:
- **Strait of Hormuz** still largely blocked — Iran released a safe-passage map but is charging ships $2M in tolls (violating international law). Traffic is below 10% of pre-war levels.
- **Oil prices** expected to stay elevated through year-end, keeping gas and airfare high.
- **Natural gas disruptions** are spiking fertilizer prices — a third of the world's nitrogen/phosphate fertilizer passes through Hormuz.
- **US GDP** for Q4 was revised down again to a sluggish 0.5%.

**Why it matters:** Even with a ceasefire, the economic ripple effects from disrupted energy supplies and shipping are baked in. Higher energy = higher everything (food, transport, manufacturing).

The PCE index (the Fed's go-to inflation metric) improved in February, but personal income dropped. The data likely keeps the Fed holding rates steady — they're stuck between cooling inflation and war-driven price shocks.

### Concept of the Day: PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures)

**What is it?** The PCE price index measures how much prices are changing for things consumers actually buy — food, healthcare, housing, clothes, everything.

**Why does the Fed prefer it over CPI?** Both measure inflation, but PCE is more comprehensive. If beef gets expensive and people switch to chicken, PCE captures that substitution. CPI doesn't — it keeps measuring beef. PCE also covers a broader range of spending (including things employers pay on your behalf, like health insurance).

**How does it connect to today's news?** February's PCE showed improvement, which normally would be great news for rate cuts. But the Iran war threw a wrench in everything — energy prices spiked after February, so the next PCE reading will likely be worse. The Fed is essentially flying blind right now, which is why they're holding rates steady and waiting.

**The key number:** The Fed targets 2% annual PCE inflation. We're still above that, and war-driven energy costs aren't helping.
