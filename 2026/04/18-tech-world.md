### Tech & AI

#### Anthropic CEO Visits White House Despite Pentagon Lawsuit
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei met with White House chief of staff Susie Wiles on Thursday, even as his company is suing over the Pentagon branding it a "supply chain risk." The visit comes as government agencies are reportedly seeking access to Anthropic's powerful **Mythos** model — which can find cybersecurity vulnerabilities faster than any human hacker. The White House appears more interested in leveraging Mythos for national security than fighting with Anthropic over procurement rules.

#### Meta Planning 8,000 Layoffs Starting May 20
Meta is preparing its largest single layoff event, targeting approximately 10% of its global workforce (~8,000 employees) in a first wave on May 20, according to Reuters. Additional cuts are planned for later in 2026. The layoffs are driven by Meta's massive AI spending pivot, with CEO Mark Zuckerberg previously stating that AI can replace entire teams. This follows several smaller rounds of cuts throughout 2025-2026 that have already eliminated thousands of positions.

#### Scale AI CEO: Companies Using AI as Excuse for Layoffs
Jason Droege, CEO of Scale AI, said at the Semafor World Economy conference that tech executives are hiding behind the excuse of AI to conduct layoffs. He argued that **AI is still too unreliable to make important human decisions**, echoing similar comments made by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent earlier in the week.

#### Mistral: Europe's AI Champion Preaches Digital Sovereignty
France's Mistral AI is positioning itself as the anti-Silicon Valley AI company. CEO Arthur Mensch proclaims "AI should be a tool for empowerment, not dominance" — a message resonating strongly as Trump's policies spark concerns about relying on American technology. Mistral has built a $14 billion AI empire by pitching itself as the alternative to US-dominated AI.

#### Cerebras Files to Go Public
AI chipmaker Cerebras, which previously scrapped IPO plans, filed new paperwork to go public. The company designs wafer-scale processors purpose-built for AI workloads and competes with Nvidia in the AI accelerator market.

### World News

#### BREAKING: Iran Reimposed Strait of Hormuz Restrictions (Saturday)
In a dramatic reversal, Iran's military announced Saturday that restrictions on the Strait of Hormuz are being reimposed, citing "repeated breaches of trust" by the United States. This comes just one day after Iran opened the strait to commercial shipping, triggering a massive market rally. Multiple outlets (CNN, Al Jazeera, The Guardian, Reuters) confirm the strait has effectively reverted to its restricted state. The US continues its blockade of Iran-affiliated traffic despite Iran's brief opening.

**Key context:**
- Iran disputes Trump's claims of agreements being reached
- The Lebanon-Israel 10-day ceasefire (brokered Thursday) is holding but nearing its end
- An American delegation could meet Iranian diplomats in Pakistan to try to finalize a deal
- Trump had claimed Iran agreed to suspend its nuclear program — Iran denies this

#### Lebanon-Israel Ceasefire: Fragile Peace Holds
The 10-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon went into effect Thursday. Tens of thousands of displaced Lebanese citizens used the first day to return home. Few on either side believe lasting peace is a realistic prospect, but the pause has provided temporary relief and was linked to Iran's brief reopening of Hormuz.

#### NYC Proposes Pied-a-Terre Tax on $5M+ Secondary Homes
New York Governor Kathy Hochul and Mayor Zohran Mamdani proposed a tax on non-primary residences valued above $5 million, targeting about 13,000 secondary homes owned by wealthy out-of-towners. Expected to generate at least $500 million annually to help close NYC's $5.4 billion budget gap. President Trump blasted the proposal, despite his generally cordial relationship with Mamdani.

#### Global Democracy Index Shows Improvement
The Economist's EIU Democracy Index suggests democracy may be turning a corner — partly thanks to Trump. Denmark's score improved after deftly handling Greenland threats. Canada avoided becoming the "51st state." Hungary's authoritarian leader Viktor Orban suffered electoral defeat after a Trump endorsement backfired. The index had declined steadily since "peak democracy" in 2015.
