## Tech & AI

### 1. Meta & Microsoft: AI In, Humans Out
The biggest tech labor story in months dropped simultaneously:
- **Meta** cutting 10% of workforce (~8,000 people), canceling 6,000 open roles. Layoffs start May 20. Reason: redirecting resources to AI.
- **Microsoft** offering voluntary buyouts to ~7% of workers. Separately committed $25B to AI in Australia and $17.9B announced at Google Cloud Next.
- The pattern is unmistakable: Big Tech is trading human headcount for AI compute.

### 2. Intel's AI Pivot Pays Off Spectacularly
Intel surged 25%+ after Q1 results destroyed expectations. Revenue hit $13.6B (vs. $12.4B expected), with Data Center & AI revenue up 22%. CEO Lip-Bu Tan declared Intel "a fundamentally different company." This isn't just about chips — it validates the broader thesis that AI infrastructure demand is still accelerating.

### 3. Software Stocks Under AI Pressure
Even companies beating estimates are getting punished. IBM and ServiceNow both reported strong Q1 numbers but fell as investors increasingly believe AI will commoditize traditional software. The market is repricing an entire sector.

### 4. OpenAI Launches GPT-5.5
OpenAI rolled out GPT-5.5, described as a significant leap in AI capabilities. Available in standard, thinking, and Pro versions. The pace of model releases continues to accelerate.

### 5. Google's AI Training Data Is... Garbage (Literally)
Google partnered with startup Mill ($1,000 "smart" trash bin company) to use a dataset on food waste for AI training. The idea: help businesses reduce food waste by understanding what's being thrown away. Food waste makes up 30-40% of the US food supply annually.

### 6. SpaceX IPO Could Eclipse Tesla
If SpaceX goes public at its rumored $1.75 trillion valuation, it would give Tesla investors "a bigger, more exciting way" to bet on Elon Musk, says Forbes. Tesla investor Ross Gerber: "There are many Tesla investors who perceive SpaceX to be a better investment."

### 7. Dan Ives: AI Trade "In Third Inning"
Wedbush's Dan Ives says the AI investment cycle is only in the third inning and remains bullish. Despite Iran-related market headwinds, he sees AI as the dominant force carrying markets forward.

## World & US News

### 1. Strait of Hormuz Standoff Deepens
The US naval blockade of Iranian ports continues with no resolution in sight. Thousands of seafarers are stranded on ships. Economic shockwaves are rippling globally — fertilizer shortages risk dragging consumer demand, and oil prices keep climbing. Iran says Russian ships can transit Hormuz free of charge.

### 2. Trump: "Clock Is Ticking" on Iran
President Trump signaled possible diplomatic progress but described talks as "slow-moving." Sources say diplomatic efforts may resume within days. The Israel-Lebanon ceasefire was extended 3 weeks.

### 3. EU Opens Door to Ukraine Membership
The European Union signaled new openness to Ukraine joining the bloc, a significant diplomatic development amid ongoing geopolitical realignment.

### 4. Soldier Charged with Insider Betting on Maduro Raid
A US special forces soldier who participated in the raid to capture Venezuelan President Maduro has been charged with using classified intel to win $400,000+ on Polymarket. He allegedly moved winnings through a foreign crypto vault before depositing them in a brokerage account.

### 5. World Cup Drama: Could Italy Replace Iran?
With geopolitics colliding with the 2026 World Cup, there's speculation about whether Iran could be excluded from the tournament. Italy reportedly rejected a Trump envoy's plan to take Iran's spot, adding a bizarre sports-diplomacy twist.

### 6. Trump Gold Card: Just 1 Approved So Far
Commerce Secretary Lutnick told lawmakers only one person has been approved for the $1M+ Trump Gold Card visa program, despite claiming $1.3 billion in sales. A lawsuit describes the program as prioritizing "wealth over intellect or ability."

### 7. Airlines Absorb Billions in Fuel Costs
All four major US airlines reported record Q1 revenue, but jet fuel surges are eating profits. American Airlines expects a $4B fuel bill increase in 2026. Delta said Q2 alone will see $2B more in fuel costs.
