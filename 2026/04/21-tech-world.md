## Tech Headlines

### 1. Tim Cook Exits -- John Ternus to Lead Apple
The biggest tech leadership change in years: Apple CEO Tim Cook steps down September 1, becoming Executive Chairman. His successor, John Ternus, has led Apple's hardware engineering division (responsible for iPhone, Mac, Vision Pro). The key challenge: can Ternus accelerate Apple's AI strategy, which analysts say has fallen behind Google, Microsoft, and Meta?

### 2. Vercel Security Breach
Vercel disclosed a security incident involving unauthorized access to internal systems. While a limited subset of customers had credentials compromised, most users (including us) were told their data appears unaffected. Vercel recommends:
- Review activity logs for suspicious behavior
- Rotate environment variables
- Enable sensitive environment variable features

This was also trending on Hacker News today.

### 3. Semiconductor Boom Continues
The SOX index hit all-time highs with a 30% gain in 13 days. TSMC's trillion-dollar revenue quarter signals unbroken AI chip demand. AMD surged 7.8% and Navitas Semiconductor jumped 20.6%. The AI infrastructure buildout shows no signs of slowing, though some analysts note the only comparable rally pattern was the March 2000 dot-com peak.

### 4. EU Mandates Replaceable Phone Batteries by 2027
All phones sold in the EU must have user-replaceable batteries starting 2027 -- a major shift for manufacturers like Apple and Samsung who have trended toward sealed designs. This was a top story on Hacker News, sparking debate about repairability vs. waterproofing and device thickness.

### 5. OpenAI Eyes $100B in Ad Revenue by 2030
OpenAI is targeting $100 billion in advertising revenue for ChatGPT by 2030 through "sponsored chats." This signals a major pivot from subscription-only models toward an ad-supported AI future, potentially reshaping digital advertising.

### 6. Hims & Hers Surges on Peptide Regulation News
FDA announced a meeting to discuss easing peptide regulation, championed by RFK Jr. Hims & Hers shares jumped 8%, as the overarching drug category includes GLP-1s and various gray-market injectables popular on social media.

## World News

### Iran Ceasefire Clock Ticking
The temporary US-Iran ceasefire expires Wednesday. President Trump says extension is "unlikely" without a peace deal. VP JD Vance is expected to travel to Pakistan for potential negotiations, though Iran hasn't confirmed participation. Trump also claimed he's "solved 9 wars" and deserves the Nobel Peace Prize -- though which conflicts are unclear, and official agreements remain pending in several.

### Three US Aircraft Carriers Heading to Middle East
The Navy is positioning three carrier strike groups in the region as the ceasefire deadline approaches, signaling both deterrence and readiness.

### Chinese Satellites Boosting Iran's War Effort
The Economist reports on how Chinese satellite imagery has filled the void left by restricted American commercial imagery, giving Iran enhanced intelligence capabilities.

### FBI Director Sues The Atlantic for $250M
Kash Patel filed a defamation lawsuit against The Atlantic and journalist Sarah Fitzpatrick over a story alleging excessive drinking and erratic behavior. The Atlantic called the suit "meritless." Proving defamation is especially difficult for public figures who must show knowing falsehood or reckless disregard for truth.

### Data Centers as Military Targets
Forbes reports that AI data centers are being viewed as strategic infrastructure targets vulnerable during wartime, driving investment in hardened security and counter-drone capabilities.

### Labor Secretary Resigns
Lori Chavez-DeRemer is leaving the Trump administration amid whistleblower claims of misconduct, including an alleged affair with a security team member and misuse of government funds.

## AI & Science Corner

### mRNA Cancer Vaccine Shows Remarkable Results
A pancreatic cancer trial showed 7 of 16 patients given personalized mRNA vaccines are still alive six years later -- remarkable for a cancer with only 13% five-year survival. The vaccine trains the immune system to fight residual cancer cells after surgery. A larger Phase 2 trial is underway, though the technology faces political headwinds from vaccine skeptics in the Trump administration.
