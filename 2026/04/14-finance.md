## Top Stories

### 1. Earnings Season Kicks Off: Goldman Sachs Posts Record, But Investors Shrug

Goldman Sachs reported its Q1 numbers to officially kick off earnings season. The investment bank's **profit jumped 19% to $5.63 billion**, beating expectations as dealmaking and market volatility fueled Goldman's second-best quarter ever for overall revenue. Despite the headline beat, the stock **fell nearly 2%** as investors were disappointed by an unexpected drop in bond-trading revenue. CEO David Solomon criticized analysts for setting the bar too high.

### 2. US Blockade of Strait of Hormuz Begins

The US officially started its blockade of ships from Iranian ports in the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz. President Trump threatened that any Iranian ships would be "immediately ELIMINATED." Iran responded that if its ports are threatened, "no port in the Persian Gulf and the Sea of Oman will be safe." However, markets were buoyed by Trump's claim that Iran had reached out to seek a peace deal (which Iran has not confirmed).

**China weighed in**, calling the blockade a "dangerous and irresponsible act," while President Xi Jinping warned that the "international order is crumbling into disarray."

The **International Energy Agency** projected global oil demand will see its sharpest decline since COVID-19, calling the ongoing supply disruption the "largest in history."

### 3. Novo Nordisk Partners with OpenAI for Drug R&D

Novo Nordisk announced a partnership with OpenAI to accelerate AI-driven drug discovery. CEO Mike Doustdar said the deal gives them "the ability to analyze datasets at a scale that was previously impossible." The stock rose over 2.5% pre-market. Analysts note Novo looks "pretty cheap right now" despite slowing growth concerns.

### 4. Bloom Energy Pops 15% on Expanded Oracle Deal

Bloom Energy surged over 15% pre-market after confirming Oracle will now procure **up to 2.8 gigawatts** of Bloom's fuel cell systems (more than doubling the original deal). The power is primarily for Oracle's AI data center projects in the US, reflecting the ongoing race among tech giants to secure fast, reliable energy for AI workloads.

### 5. United CEO Allegedly Pitches Mega-Merger with American Airlines

United Airlines CEO Scott Kirby reportedly pitched President Trump on merging with American Airlines, which would create the world's largest airline. The two were already the largest by available capacity in 2025. Any deal would face massive regulatory hurdles from unions, rivals, and lawmakers.

### 6. Hollywood Protests Paramount-Warner Merger

Over 1,000 Hollywood creators, including Ben Stiller, Joaquin Phoenix, and Kristen Stewart, issued a statement opposing Paramount's planned $111 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery. They worry the merger would reduce US major film studios to just four and lead to fewer movie releases and job losses. The number of entertainment industry jobs has already dropped 30% since late 2022.

## The Sneaky Side of Inflation

Businesses are passing along higher costs as separate line items rather than raising menu prices directly. Examples include credit card surcharges at restaurants, increased baggage fees from multiple airlines (to offset rising jet fuel prices), and hidden service fees. Forbes notes that even when the temporary justification is resolved, don't expect costs to come down - Delta's CEO noted lower fuel costs would "boost margins," not reduce fares.

## Concept of the Day: The 60/40 Portfolio

The **60/40 portfolio** is one of the most classic investment strategies: put 60% of your money in stocks and 40% in bonds. The idea is simple - stocks provide growth while bonds provide stability, and when stocks go down, bonds typically go up (and vice versa), smoothing your returns.

**Why it matters today:** The strategy has faced criticism in recent years because in 2022, both stocks AND bonds fell at the same time (something that rarely happens), making investors question whether the 60/40 still works. With Treasury yields around 4.3% and stocks at all-time highs, the debate continues. Some argue bonds finally offer attractive yields again after years of near-zero rates, making the 60/40 relevant once more. Others say you need to add alternatives like commodities or real estate to truly diversify in today's market.

**The takeaway:** If someone asks about a "balanced portfolio," they're usually talking about some version of 60/40. It's a good starting framework, but many modern advisors suggest tweaking the ratio based on your age, risk tolerance, and market conditions.
