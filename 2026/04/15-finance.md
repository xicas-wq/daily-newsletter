## Top Stories

### 1. Markets Rally Hard on Iran Peace Hopes

Stocks surged Monday as investors grew optimistic about a potential resolution to the Iran conflict. The Nasdaq led gains at nearly 2%, while the S&P 500 pushed close to record territory. Key drivers:
- Several Iran-linked ships appeared to pass through the Strait of Hormuz (though the US says its blockade held)
- Another round of in-person US-Iran talks could happen as early as this week
- Oil prices dropped, easing inflation fears

**Why it matters:** Citadel CEO Ken Griffin warned that a recession is "inevitable" if the Strait of Hormuz stays closed for 6-12 months. Markets are betting that won't happen.

### 2. JPMorgan Crushes Earnings

JPMorgan Chase delivered a blockbuster Q1:
- Revenue grew 10% to nearly **$50 billion**, beating Wall Street expectations
- Trading fees hit a **record high**
- M&A advising fees surged **82%**
- Investment banking fees rose more than any other global bank

CEO Jamie Dimon struck a cautious tone despite the strong results, warning about "geopolitical tensions and wars" and "energy price volatility" ahead. The bank slightly revised down full-year guidance for net-interest income.

**Elsewhere in banking:** Citigroup also beat expectations on strong trading, while Wells Fargo fell short.

### 3. Fed Nominee Kevin Warsh: The Wealthiest Chair Ever?

Financial disclosures show Trump's pick to replace Jerome Powell is worth between **$135M-$226M** — significantly richer than Powell ($19M-$75M). Warsh said he'd divest from conflicting assets. His wife, Jane Lauder (Estee Lauder heir), is worth an estimated $1.9 billion. His nomination hearing is next Tuesday.

### 4. United Airlines Floats Merger with American

United CEO Scott Kirby reportedly pitched the idea of acquiring American Airlines to President Trump. The combined entity would be the world's largest airline, controlling over 40% of the domestic market. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy seemed open to the idea, but antitrust experts are skeptical — the airlines overlap on hundreds of routes.

**What to watch:** Airfare could rise significantly if competition is reduced. Expect heavy regulatory scrutiny.

### 5. Amazon Goes All-In on Satellite Internet

Amazon is buying satellite company **Globalstar for $11.6 billion** to expand its Leo satellite internet service, directly challenging SpaceX's Starlink. Separately, Boeing delivered more jets than Airbus in Q1, continuing its post-crisis comeback.

## Tax Day Special

It's April 15, and this year's filing season has an unusual twist: **taxpayers are less afraid of the IRS than ever.**

- The IRS started 2025 with 102,000 employees; it ended the year with ~74,000 after cuts and buyouts
- The White House clawed back ~$54 billion of the $79.4B earmarked for IRS enforcement
- Audits hit a historic low of 497,541 last year (down from 1.7M/year average in 2010-2012)
- Tax pros say more filers are "fudging" numbers, betting the IRS won't catch them

**The tradeoff:** While IRS cuts save an estimated $46B over a decade, they could cost **$643B in lost revenue**, per Yale's Budget Lab. New deductions from Trump's One Big Beautiful Bill increased the average refund by 11% to $3,462.

## Concept of the Day: The Strait of Hormuz

The **Strait of Hormuz** is a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman — roughly 21 miles wide at its narrowest point. About **20-25% of the world's oil** passes through it daily, making it one of the most strategically important chokepoints on Earth.

**Why it's in the news:** The US has imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports in the Strait as part of the Iran conflict. If this blockade continues long-term, oil supply disruptions could push prices dramatically higher, raising gas prices, increasing inflation, and potentially triggering a recession. That's why markets react so strongly to any news about peace talks — a resolution would reopen this critical oil artery.

**Connection to today:** The market rally we saw Monday is directly tied to hopes that the Strait situation will be resolved soon. When peace talk optimism grows, oil prices drop, and stocks rise. When tensions escalate, the reverse happens. The Strait of Hormuz is essentially the barometer of global market sentiment right now.
